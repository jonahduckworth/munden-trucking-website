## Dust and debris can choke airflow faster than people expect
Trucks that spend their days on gravel roads, in construction zones, around milling sites, or in forestry work see a steady load of dust, chaff, and fine debris. That material does not just sit on the surface. It works its way into the radiator, charge air cooler, and condenser stack and starts restricting airflow.

When airflow drops, cooling gets less efficient. Drivers may notice higher coolant temps, a hotter charge air system, more frequent fan engagement, or a truck that runs fine on the highway but starts to climb in temperature under load or at low speed. In Kamloops and across the BC Interior, that is a common pattern on trucks that work hard in dusty conditions.

A quick visual check can tell you a lot. If the fins look packed with dirt, bugs, seed fluff, or oily road film, cleaning may be due. ## Safe cleaning matters as much as the cleaning itself
A cooling stack can be damaged easily if it is washed the wrong way. High pressure too close to the fins can bend them over and make airflow worse. Harsh chemicals can also leave residue or affect nearby components if they are not used carefully.

Good cleaning starts with the right approach for the truck and the condition of the stack:

- Inspect the fins before washing so you can see what is actually plugged.
- Check for bent or folded fins that may need straightening.
- Clean from the correct side when possible so debris comes out instead of deeper in.
- Avoid forcing dirt into the stack with excessive pressure.
- Look for oil leaks or coolant seepage that could keep the core dirty.

If a truck works in brush, dust, or winter road spray, a cleaning interval should be tied to operating conditions instead of just mileage alone. That is especially true for commercial truck maintenance in BC, where terrain and work type can change how fast a cooling stack loads up.

## What repeated hot running may be telling you
A dirty radiator is not always the whole story. If temperatures keep rising after cleaning, there may be another issue underneath the symptom. Bent fins, damaged shrouding, loose mounts, fan problems, charge air leaks, or debris trapped between stacked coolers can all affect performance.

Watch for patterns:

- Temps rise mainly on grades or in stop-and-go work.
- The problem shows up after recent washouts or off-road jobs.
- The truck cools down at speed but not at idle.
- The fan seems to run harder than normal.
- You see oil, coolant, or debris stuck in the same area again and again.

Those clues help a technician decide whether the fix is cleaning, fin repair, hose inspection, or a deeper look at the cooling system. ## A practical habit for fleets and owner-operators
For fleet owners, drivers, and contractors, the best approach is simple: make cooling stack checks part of the regular walkaround and service routine. A few minutes spent looking at the radiator and charge air cooler can save a long day of hot-running trouble later.

That is especially true for equipment working around dust, debris, and variable load. If you are also tracking other wear items, this is a good time to bundle the inspection with related service so the truck is not pulled apart twice. If a truck starts running hot, do not wait for the gauge to make the decision for you. Catch the airflow problem early, clean it properly, and check whether the fins, leaks, or mounting points are part of the real issue.
