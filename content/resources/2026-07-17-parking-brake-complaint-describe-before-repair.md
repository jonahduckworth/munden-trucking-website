## Why parking brake complaints are worth describing clearly
A parking brake problem is one of those complaints that can sound simple at the counter and turn out to be a few different issues in the shop. A driver may say the brake “doesn’t hold,” “takes too much pull,” “drags when released,” or “acts different on a hill.” Those details matter.

For fleet owners and drivers in Kamloops and across the BC Interior, the more clearly a complaint is described, the easier it is to narrow down whether the concern is with holding ability, release behaviour, warning indicators, linkage, air system condition, or something else in the brake system. That helps the shop focus the inspection and avoid guessing.

If you are lining up [service work](/services/service-department), start by writing down exactly what changed and when it happened.

## What to note before you bring it in
Try to capture the complaint while it is still fresh. A few simple notes can go a long way:

- Whether the truck, trailer, or both are involved
- If the problem shows up on flat ground, a slope, or both
- How much force or travel is needed for the brake to hold
- Whether the brake releases normally or seems to stay on
- Any dash warning lights, air loss, or unusual sounds
- Whether the issue started after another repair, wash, adjustment, or inspection
- Any load-related change, such as the complaint being worse empty or loaded

For a driver, that might sound like: “It held on level ground yesterday, but today it rolled slightly on a grade with the same setup.” That is much more useful than “parking brake bad.”

If the truck is part of a repeat concern, include the unit number, recent work history, and any photos of the brake components, linkage, or warning message. Even a quick phone picture can help a technician understand what they will be looking at before the unit arrives.

## Common clues that should be mentioned early
Some parking brake complaints point the shop in a better direction right away:

- The brake holds but releases slowly
- The brake applies, but the truck creeps on a slope
- The control feels different than usual
- The dash shows an air system warning at the same time
- The brake behaves differently after the vehicle has sat
- The linkage or hardware looks damaged, bent, or out of place

Those details do not diagnose the issue by themselves, but they help separate a linkage problem from an air supply issue, a chamber problem, or a mechanical adjustment concern. That is especially useful on commercial trucks and trailers that see stop-and-go work, grades, dirt roads, or long idle periods in the Interior.

## How better driver notes help the repair process
Clear notes help the shop decide what to inspect first, which saves time during a busy week. They also help when the vehicle is coming in for a CVIP inspection or a general maintenance visit, because the technician can look at the complaint alongside the rest of the brake system condition.

That is one reason fleets that keep good driver reports usually have fewer back-and-forths. When a service advisor can match the driver’s description with the unit history, the inspection starts with a better picture of the problem.

If your parking brake complaint is not urgent but it is changing, it is still worth booking sooner rather than later. A small change in holding or release behaviour can be a sign that the system needs attention before it becomes a roadside issue.
