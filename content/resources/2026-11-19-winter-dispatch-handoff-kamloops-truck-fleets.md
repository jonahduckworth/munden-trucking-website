## Put the route and the truck in the same conversation

For a truck leaving Kamloops, a winter departure plan needs more than a destination and an expected arrival time. The person assigning the trip, the driver and the person managing maintenance should be working from the same information. A route may be available while a vehicle still has an unresolved concern; a recently serviced vehicle may still need to wait for safer travel conditions.

[WorkSafeBC's winter guidance](https://www.worksafebc.com/en/about-us/news-events/campaigns/shift-into-winter) recommends postponing travel when conditions are poor if possible, checking the route when travel is necessary and allowing time to avoid rushing. That is a useful starting point for a short dispatch conversation, not a substitute for a carrier's safety procedures or the driver's judgment.

The checklist below is a suggested way to organize that conversation. It is not a mechanical inspection checklist, a legal compliance guide or permission to operate a truck with a defect.

## Make a route decision before setting the arrival promise

Check [DriveBC](https://www.drivebc.ca/) and the relevant weather forecast before departure. Review the whole intended route rather than assuming conditions at the Kamloops yard describe conditions farther along the trip. Record when the information was checked so the next person knows whether it needs refreshing.

A practical dispatch note can answer four questions:

- What route is planned, and who will review it if conditions change?
- Where can the driver safely stop to reassess the trip?
- Who needs to know if departure or arrival is delayed?
- Who has responsibility for deciding whether the trip should be postponed?

Do not turn an estimated arrival into a reason to continue through unsafe conditions. If the plan changes, update the people relying on it rather than leaving the driver to reconcile an outdated schedule on the road.

## Close the loop on service notes

Keep trip planning separate from the vehicle's required inspections, but connect the information. Before assigning the unit, confirm that outstanding driver reports have been reviewed through the fleet's maintenance process. A note marked “reported” should not be mistaken for a repair marked complete.

For an unresolved concern, give the service team the unit number, the driver's description and any relevant previous work order. Ask what assessment is needed before dispatch. Avoid diagnosing a fault from a short message or treating a warning that disappears as proof that the underlying problem has been resolved.

If maintenance needs attention before the next trip, contact the [Munden service department](/services/service-department) with those details. This helps frame the discussion around the actual vehicle and concern rather than a general request to “winterize it.” Confirm the scope and timing directly; a booking request is not a completed inspection.

## Agree on a check-in plan

[Transport Canada](https://tc.canada.ca/en/road-transportation/stay-safe-when-driving/winter-driving/planning-your-trip) advises sharing the route and expected arrival, carrying a charged phone and planning for unexpected delays. For a fleet, translate that into a named contact and an agreed check-in procedure that fits the operation.

Before departure, confirm that the driver knows whom to contact, what to do if the plan changes and which company emergency procedure applies. Review winter supplies against the vehicle's needs and the carrier's requirements. Make those preparations while parked, not while driving.

## Leave a useful record for the next shift

A short handoff can capture the unit, planned route, latest conditions check, maintenance clearance status and next check-in. Add changes as they happen. The goal is not another form for its own sake: it is a shared picture of what has been checked, what remains unresolved and who owns the next decision.

For Kamloops and BC Interior fleets, that clear division of responsibility is worth building into the routine before the departure queue gets busy.
