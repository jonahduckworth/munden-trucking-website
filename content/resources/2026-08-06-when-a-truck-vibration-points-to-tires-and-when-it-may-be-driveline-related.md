## Start with the speed where the vibration shows up
A truck vibration is easier to sort out when you can say exactly when it starts. Does it show up at 45 km/h, 80 km/h, or only under highway load? Does it happen in every gear, or only when the truck is pulling hard? Those details matter because tire-related issues and driveline issues often show up under different conditions.

If the vibration is strongest at a certain road speed and stays fairly steady, tires, wheels, or wheel-end condition are often part of the conversation. If it changes more with throttle input, load, or gear selection, the driveline may deserve a closer look. That does not confirm the cause on its own, but it gives the shop a better starting point.

For fleets working around the Interior, those details are worth writing down before the unit heads in for [service department review](/services/service-department).

## Clues that lean toward tires or wheels
Recent tire work is a big clue. If a vibration started after a tire replacement, rotation, repair, or wheel change, that should be part of the note. Even when the tires look fine at a glance, a problem can come from uneven wear, a damaged casing, a bent wheel, or pressure issues.

A driver can help by noting:
- Whether the vibration is felt in the seat, steering wheel, or floor
- Whether it gets better or worse with speed
- Whether it changes after a load change
- Whether any tire was recently replaced, repaired, or moved
- Whether there is a new hum, thump, or rhythm that matches the vibration

If the truck has just come back from tire service, do not assume the problem is solved because the tread looks acceptable. A simple note like “started after rear axle tire swap” can save time and avoid extra road testing.

## Clues that point more toward the driveline
Driveline-related vibration often has a different feel. It may come with a clunk on takeoff, a change when the truck shifts under load, or a vibration that is more noticeable during acceleration than at cruise. Universal joints, slip yokes, carrier bearings, driveshaft balance, and mount condition can all play a role.

Watch for these signs:
- Vibration that appears mainly under throttle
- A change when backing off the pedal
- New clunks, squeaks, or binding
- A vibration that started after a driveline repair, suspension repair, or impact
- Visible movement, leaks, or damaged mounts around the affected area

For logging trucks and other heavy vocational units, repeated rough-road travel can add stress to the same parts that keep the driveline aligned and stable. That is one reason a good walkaround note helps before a road test or inspection.

## What to tell the shop before the truck arrives
The best shop notes are plain and specific. A short message with the speed range, load condition, recent tire work, and where the vibration is felt gives the technician a much better head start. Photos help too, especially if a tire shows odd wear, a wheel looks damaged, or there is an obvious leak near a driveline component.

If you want to make the visit smoother, bring:
- Unit number and mileage or hours
- When the vibration started
- Whether it happens loaded, empty, or both
- Any recent tire, wheel, suspension, or driveline work
- Photos of worn tires, leaks, or visible damage

That kind of detail helps with Kamloops truck repair planning, trailer repair follow-up, and mobile truck service calls when a unit needs attention before it can keep working. It also supports cleaner documentation ahead of a CVIP inspection Kamloops operators may already be scheduling.

## Keep the note simple, but do not skip it
A vibration is one of those problems that can waste time if the description is too vague. “Shakes at speed” is better than nothing, but “vibration starts around 72 km/h, feels strongest in the seat, and got worse after a rear tire repair” is much more useful.

If the issue turns out to be tire-related, driveline-related, or a mix of both, the first good note usually shortens the path to repair. That is good for drivers, dispatch, and anyone trying to keep commercial truck maintenance in BC on schedule without unnecessary downtime.
